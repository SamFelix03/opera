What Opera is

Beyond tokenization

Existing RWA platforms stop at issuance. Opera models everything after — who may manage the asset, collect from it, distribute revenue, and whether that authority is still valid.

The core instrument is a Living Operating Right (LOR): programmable authority over asset operations, continuously repriced by the holder’s Cleanverse compliance score. Compliance is not a gate — it is the pricing engine.

The problem

  • Operators and distributors are managed off-chain, with no live compliance enforcement
  • Whitelist status is binary — a degraded operator keeps full authority until a human revokes it
  • Compliance has no economic consequence beyond manual removal

What Opera does

  • Adds a programmable operational layer on tokenized RWAs
  • Mediates rent, maintenance, leases, and distributions through LORs
  • Reprices yield, transfer cost, and listing status from a live compliance score

How Opera is different

Most RWA platforms only track ownership. Opera tracks who is currently allowed to operate the asset, and ties that permission to live Cleanverse compliance.

Traditional RWA

  • Ask: who owns this asset?
  • Compliance checked at transfer
  • Delegation is static
  • Agents only report
  • Compliance is a gate

Opera

  • Ask: who is currently authorized to operate it?
  • Compliance reprices authority continuously
  • Authority is economically alive
  • Agents are market participants with staked capital
  • Compliance is a pricing engine
